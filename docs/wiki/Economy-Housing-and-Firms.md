# Economy, Housing & Firms

[← Wiki Home](Home.md)

## Current baseline

**Status: Playable and largely Transitional.**

The current compatibility layer includes population, employment, taxation, treasury, utilities, firms, production, inventories, imports/exports, freight, housing affordability, renter/owner tenure, persistent relocation, developer capital allocation, redevelopment safeguards, and related municipal-service interactions.

Urban Fabric 2.0 already supplies canonical parcel/building/property foundations beneath this layer.

## Economy 2.0 target

The mature economy is intended to use configurable sectors linked by input-output production. Firms consume intermediate inputs, labor, capital, energy, and logistics rather than producing from disconnected global bonuses.

Firm accounts can track revenue, COGS, payroll, rent, utilities, logistics, taxes, interest, capex, cash, debt, and profit/loss. Material transfers should reconcile through an economic ledger.

Labor matching should consider skills, occupation, location/accessibility, wages, available workers, and employer demand. Firms may form, hire, expand, contract, relocate, automate, become distressed, recover, or close.

Supplier choice should consider delivered generalized cost, including production price, distance, congestion, reliability, and inventory risk.

## Real-estate capitalism target

Properties may be owned by households, landlords, developers, firms, institutional investors, or government. Future systems can model explicit/weighted housing units, rental listings and leases, for-sale markets, comparables/appraisal, mortgages, developer underwriting, lenders, contractors, approvals, construction capacity, and displacement safeguards.

Developer finance may include acquisition, demolition, hard/soft cost, fees, financing, interest carry, schedule, lease-up/sales, NOI, value, debt/equity, IRR, equity multiple, and lender constraints.

## Households and demographics target

Long-term state should move beyond one aggregate population count toward weighted people/cohorts and households with age, education, occupation, employment, income, health, tenure, vehicles, savings/debt, dependents, preferences, and relocation constraints.

Lifecycle processes can include schooling, graduation, household formation, employment changes, migration, retirement, birth, and death.

## Cross-domain invariants

Money should reconcile; inventory cannot go negative; labor allocation cannot exceed workers/jobs; housing occupancy cannot exceed capacity; property ownership cannot conflict; developer capital cannot be spent twice; and congestion should affect delivered cost where relevant.
